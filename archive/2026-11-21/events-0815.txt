EN: China News Service, November 20. Data released by Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications on the 20th showed that after excluding fresh food, Japan's core consumer price index (CPI) rose by 2.2% year-on-year in October, exceeding the 2% target level set by the Bank of Japan for two consecutive months. Japanese media pointed out that as of October, Japan’s core CPI had risen year-on-year for 62 consecutive months. Data show that rising energy prices are an important factor driving up price increases in Japan. Japan's energy prices have risen due to factors such as government cuts in subsidies for electricity and city gas bills, as well as tensions in the Middle East pushing up crude oil prices.
