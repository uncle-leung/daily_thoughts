ENChina News Service, November 10. According to the British Broadcasting Corporation (BBC), an explosion occurred at a steel plant in Izmir Province in western Turkey on the 9th local time. The incident killed 17 people and injured 12 others. According to reports, the explosion caused a fire and thick smoke billowed from the accident site. Witnesses of the accident said that the vibrations from the explosion were so strong that they were mistaken for an earthquake. According to reports, Turkish President Erdogan initially said that the number of victims had risen to 16. ENOn November 9, local time, the latest search and rescue situation announced by the relevant departments of Senegal that day showed that a shipwreck in the waters near Kebermer Province in the Luga region in the north of the country had killed 39 people. Rescuers conducted searches along a coastal area of ​​approximately 25 kilometers for three consecutive days. ENOn November 9, local time, the U.S. Treasury Department released data stating that in the 2026 fiscal year ending on September 30, the U.S. federal budget deficit totaled $2.0 trillion, an increase of $217 billion, or 12%, from the deficit in the previous fiscal year. Both revenue and expenditure increased compared with fiscal year 2025. Revenue increased 3%, or $169 billion; expenses increased 6%, or $386 billion. In addition, the federal deficit in fiscal year 2026 is equivalent to 6.2% of gross domestic product (GDP), up from 5.8% in fiscal 2025.

ENChina News Service, London, November 9 (Reporter Ouyang Kaiyu) Thames Water, the UK's largest water company, announced on the 9th the launch of a new round of priority debt financing arrangements. According to this financing plan, Thames Water has obtained permission from its creditors to ensure the continued advancement of basic water investment projects such as pipe network reconstruction and sewage treatment. Thames Water currently carries about 20 billion pounds (approximately 179.1 billion yuan) of debt. As an important public utility enterprise serving London and southeastern England, it has been plagued by high debt for a long time in recent years. Coupled with problems such as insufficient investment in infrastructure and illegal discharge of sewage, it has frequently been subject to regulatory penalties and operational pressure has continued to increase.
