1. 中国气象局：今年台风个数创21世纪新高
China Meteorological Administration: The number of typhoons this year hit a new high in the 21st century

中新网11月9日电 据中国气象局微信公众号消息，11月7日14时，2026年第32号台风“查帕卡”在西北太平洋洋面生成。台风“查帕卡”的生成，使2026年的台风生成数量创下21世纪以来最高纪录。回顾21世纪以来的台风数据，西北太平洋和南海平均每年生成台风24.3个，多数年份台风生成个数在20个至30个之间浮动。多年统计中，只有2004年、2013年两年台风生成数量突破30个，其中2013年生成31个台风，是此前台风生成数量的最高纪录，台风“查帕卡”成为21世纪以来首个“第32号台风”。

ENChina News Service, November 9. According to the China Meteorological Administration’s WeChat official account, at 14:00 on November 7, the 2026 Typhoon No. 32 "Chapaca" was generated in the northwest Pacific Ocean. The formation of Typhoon "Chapaka" will set the number of typhoons in 2026 to the highest record since the 21st century. Looking back at typhoon data since the 21st century, an average of 24.3 typhoons are generated in the northwest Pacific and South China Sea every year, and the number of typhoons fluctuates between 20 and 30 in most years. In many years of statistics, only in 2004 and 2013 did the number of typhoons exceed 30. Among them, 31 typhoons were generated in 2013, which was the previous record for the number of typhoons. Typhoon "Chapaca" became the first "Typhoon No. 32" in the 21st century.

10. 世界气象组织：近四年为有记录以来最热的四年
World Meteorological Organization: The past four years have been the hottest on record

根据世界气象组织公布的最新数据，2026年1月至9月，全球近地表平均气温较工业化前(1850年至1900年)水平高出1.47℃，与2023年和2025年全年水平相当，低于2024年创下的1.55℃纪录。2023年至2026年成为有记录以来最热的四年。报告指出，年初的拉尼娜现象曾短暂抑制全球升温，但随着厄尔尼诺现象发展，全球气温再次攀升。预计厄尔尼诺将持续至2027年初，可能进一步推高2026年全年平均气温。

ENAccording to the latest data released by the World Meteorological Organization, from January to September 2026, the global average near-surface temperature was 1.47°C higher than the pre-industrial level (1850 to 1900), which was equivalent to the full-year levels in 2023 and 2025, and lower than the record of 1.55°C set in 2024. 2023 to 2026 become the four hottest years on record. The report pointed out that the La Niña phenomenon at the beginning of the year briefly suppressed global warming, but as the El Niño phenomenon developed, global temperatures climbed again. El Niño is expected to last until early 2027, which may further push up the average temperature throughout 2026.
