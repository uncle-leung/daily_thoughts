ENOriginal title: Authoritative Express from Xinhua News Agency | China plans to amend the Criminal Procedure Law to better promote judicial justice. The draft amendment to the Criminal Procedure Law was submitted to the 25th meeting of the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress for review on November 2. The main content to be revised this time includes seven aspects: improving the basic principles of criminal proceedings, improving physical coercion measures, improving the procedures for the seizure, seizure, freezing and disposal of property involved in the case, further protecting the rights of litigation participants, improving the implementation mechanism of the Constitution's provisions on the division of responsibilities, mutual cooperation and mutual restraint of the people's courts, people's procuratorates and public security organs, improving the judicial procedures for minors' criminal cases, and improving the foreign-related criminal litigation system.

6. 考古学家：史前治水是长江文明鲜明特征 彰显先民生存智慧
Archaeologist: Prehistoric water control is a distinctive feature of the Yangtze River civilization, demonstrating the survival wisdom of the ancestors

中新社湖北荆门11月1日电 (记者 孙自法)中国社会科学院考古研究所所长施劲松研究员11月1日表示，考古发现和研究表明，史前治水是长江文明的鲜明特征，对于水资源的利用和管理，集中彰显史前先民顺应自然、改造自然的生存智慧，也深刻影响早期聚落形态的演变、社会组织的发展乃至区域文明化的进程。由中国社会科学院考古研究所、中国考古学会新石器考古专业委员会、湖北省文物考古研究院共同主办的史前治水与长江文明溯源学术研讨会，当天在素有荆楚门户之称的湖北荆门举行，施劲松出席会议并致辞。他说，自古以来，人类逐水而居，从挖渠耕种、开壕筑坝、发展农业，再到跨流域的防洪、运输，水资源的管控和使用能力始终与社会发展紧密相连。考古研究还表明，史前治水绝非是单纯的生产技术活动，它关联着资源调配、社会组织、公共权力、技术传承、信仰体系等，深入研究史前治水遗存，可以从多重维度来探究早期社会结构和文明起源、发展的模式。

ENChina News Service, Jingmen, Hubei Province, November 1 (Reporter Sun Zifa) Researcher Shi Jinsong, director of the Institute of Archeology of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, said on November 1 that archaeological discoveries and research have shown that prehistoric water control is a distinctive feature of the Yangtze River civilization. The utilization and management of water resources reflect the survival wisdom of prehistoric ancestors in adapting to and transforming nature. It also profoundly affects the evolution of early settlement forms, the development of social organizations and even the process of regional civilization. An academic seminar on prehistoric water control and traceability of the Yangtze River civilization, co-sponsored by the Institute of Archeology of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, the Neolithic Archeology Professional Committee of the Chinese Archaeological Society, and the Hubei Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archeology, was held that day in Jingmen, Hubei, known as the gateway to Jingchu. Shi Jinsong attended the meeting and delivered a speech. He said that since ancient times, human beings have lived by water. From digging canals for farming, digging trenches and dams, developing agriculture, to flood control and transportation across river basins, the ability to manage, control and use water resources has always been closely linked to social development. Archaeological research also shows that prehistoric water control is by no means a simple production technology activity. It is related to resource allocation, social organization, public power, technological inheritance, belief systems, etc. In-depth study of the remains of prehistoric water control can explore the early social structure and the origin and development model of civilization from multiple dimensions.
